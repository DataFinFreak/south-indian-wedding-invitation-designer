## 1. Ground rules

- **Treat everything here as editable defaults, not rules.** Families differ by community, sub-sect and village.
- **VERIFY flags.** Items marked **[VERIFY]** come from synthesis or practitioner knowledge, not an authoritative source. Before using them in a final invite, tell the user: "Please check this with a native speaker or your family priest."
- **Never machine-translate silently.** Do not convert English lines into a regional language on your own. If the user wants a regional line, ask them to supply it, or offer a draft clearly labelled "draft, verify with a native speaker" and use it only after they approve it.
- **Use the family's own event names.** Do not replace "Aarathakshate" with "Reception" (or the reverse) unless asked.
- **Print auspicious times exactly.** Copy muhurtham and lagna windows character for character. Add panchanga details (samvatsara, masa, tithi, nakshatra) only if the user supplies them.
- **Religious lines are opt-in.** No invocation, deity name or emblem unless the user asks.

---

## 2. Questions to ask first

Ask one at a time, each with a recommended answer:

1. Whose family is sending this invite? (Recommended for a friends-only e-invite: "the couple".) The host family's child is usually named first; offer two versions if both families send their own.
2. Which region and language tradition? Is the invite English-only, or should it carry one regional line?
3. Which events, using the names your family uses?
4. Do you want an invocation line or deity emblem? (Recommended: none for a digital invite to friends.)
5. Do you want family or elder names? (Recommended: no, for a friends-only e-invite.)

**Typical card order [VERIFY]:** invocation (optional) → blessings of elders (deceased elders often marked "Late") → host parents requesting presence → couple with "son of / daughter of" lines → muhurtha line (date, weekday, exact time window, lagna) → venue (kalyana mantapa name, address, map) → programme of events → compliments line from relatives → optional "kindly confirm on WhatsApp" line.

Common motifs: kalasha, mango-leaf torana, Mysuru palace and Dasara elephants, mallige, Mysore silk zari.

| Term | Meaning | Source status |
|---|---|---|

| Varaverpu | Reception | [VERIFY] |
| Selvi / Selvan, Chiranjeevi | Honorifics for bride / groom | [VERIFY] |

**Conventions:** traditional cards open with the family deity, then lineage back to grandparents (family temple, native place), then the muhurtham per astrology. Turmeric-yellow grounds are common and auspicious. A parallel English card is common, with only a few traditional copies printed for elders. Modern cards often put the programme in a separate block with the muhurtham window and lagnam. (Sourced; the Tamil terms above are [VERIFY].)

Common motifs: Kanjeevaram temple border, mango (maanga), kolam, gopuram silhouette, peacock.

## 6. Kerala / Malayalam

- **Aesthetic:** cream and gold kasavu is the regional norm (sourced). White and gold is auspicious here, so the "avoid white" caution does not apply to kasavu cream.
- **Muhurtham:** calculated to the minute by the family priest (sourced). Print it exactly.
- **Temple weddings [VERIFY]:** short ceremonies; the venue line names the temple. Cards are restrained, with nilavilakku (brass lamp) and jasmine motifs.
- **Malayalam terms:** none verified in research. Ask the user for every Malayalam word.

## 7. Other faiths and interfaith weddings

- Christian and Muslim weddings in Kerala, Tamil Nadu and Karnataka use different structures (church service or nikah time, different event names). Do not apply a Hindu template.
- Ask: "What are the ceremonies called, and what should the time line say?" Use the user's words exactly.
- For interfaith weddings, offer a neutral design with no religious motif unless both families ask for one.

## 8. Wording structures with placeholders

Placeholders: [BRIDE_NAME], [GROOM_NAME], [WEDDING_DATE], [MUHURTHAM_TIME], [VENUE_NAME], [VENUE_ADDRESS], [FAMILY_DETAILS]. Replace each only with what the user supplies. If a value is missing, leave the placeholder visible and ask.

**Structure A: couple-hosted digital invite (default for friends)**

1. Eyebrow: "Together with our families"
2. Names: [GROOM_NAME] & [BRIDE_NAME] (order as the user wants)
3. Invitation line
4. Events: one card per event (title, [WEDDING_DATE], [MUHURTHAM_TIME] or the event's time text)
5. Venue: [VENUE_NAME], [VENUE_ADDRESS]
6. Closing line and countdown

**Structure B: family-hosted traditional order [VERIFY order with the family]**

1. Invocation (only if requested)
2. With the blessings of [FAMILY_DETAILS] (elders, exactly as supplied)
3. Host parents request your presence
4. Couple: [BRIDE_NAME] (daughter of ...) with [GROOM_NAME] (son of ...), honorifics only as supplied
5. Muhurtham: [WEDDING_DATE], weekday, [MUHURTHAM_TIME], lagna if supplied
6. Venue: [VENUE_NAME], [VENUE_ADDRESS]
7. Programme of other events
8. Compliments line (only if supplied)

In the template, structure B's elder and parent lines go into `familyDetails` as one plain string the user approves. Keep it short on mobile.

**Structure C: bilingual**

Regional line first and English second (English at 70 to 80 percent size), or a separate language version. Never mix scripts within one line except for names. The template's `secondaryScript.invitationLine` holds one approved regional line, shown in the hero directly below the English invitation line (the template does not put the regional line first). Section labels and buttons stay in English unless you edit the template's strings with supplied or approved wording.

## 9. English wording templates

Offer the tone choice (formal, warm, minimal) with warm as the recommendation. These are starting points; the user must approve the final lines.

**Formal**
- Eyebrow: "With the blessings of our elders"
- Invitation line: "request the honour of your presence at the wedding of"
- Event line: "Muhurtham on [WEDDING_DATE] at [MUHURTHAM_TIME]"
- Closing: "Your presence and blessings are requested"

**Warm (recommended)**
- Eyebrow: "Together with our families"
- Invitation line: "We joyfully invite you to celebrate our wedding"
- Event line: "Muhurtham, [WEDDING_DATE], [MUHURTHAM_TIME]"
- Closing: "Your presence is the greatest blessing"

**Minimal**
- Eyebrow: "Save the date"
- Invitation line: "are getting married"
- Event line: "[WEDDING_DATE] · [VENUE_NAME]"
- Closing: "See you there"

Do not add taglines, stories, hashtags, "dinner and music" lines or anything the user did not approve.

## 10. Script fonts and typesetting rules

| Script | `lang` | Template default | Alternatives (OFL) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kannada | kn | Noto Serif Kannada | Tiro Kannada (classical), Baloo Tamma 2 (rounded, casual), Anek Kannada (contemporary) |
| Tamil | ta | Noto Serif Tamil | Tiro Tamil, Hind Madurai (sans) |
| Telugu | te | Noto Serif Telugu | Tiro Telugu, Ponnala (display) |
| Malayalam | ml | Noto Serif Malayalam | Manjari (rounded), Gayathri (sans) |

Typesetting rules for Indic text:
- Line-height 1.5 to 1.7 (stacked conjuncts and vowel signs need room).
- Never letter-space, uppercase-transform or faux-italicise Indic text.
- Body size at least 17 to 18 px on mobile; never below 14 px.
- Mark each regional span with its `lang` attribute; the template does this from `secondaryScript.lang`.
- Pair by similar stroke contrast: Noto Serif Kannada with Cormorant Garamond; Anek Kannada with Anek Latin; Baloo Tamma 2 with Baloo 2.
